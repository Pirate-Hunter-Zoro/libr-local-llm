%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The idea, in one sentence}

\goodbox{\large Put an AI assistant \textbf{on the lab's own computers}, so several people can use
it at once --- and so it is allowed to read patient data, which no outside service ever will be.}

\vspace{1.0em}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
{\large\textbf{Today}}\\[0.4em]
{\small You use Claude. It is excellent and fast. But every word you send leaves the building, so
it can never touch a patient recording or transcript.}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
{\large\textbf{What this would add}}\\[0.4em]
{\small An assistant living on our own machines. A bit slower. Not quite as clever. But it can look
at the actual data, because nothing ever leaves.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{1.0em}
\keybox{This is \textbf{not} a replacement for Claude. It is a second tool that can go where Claude
is not allowed to go.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{First, the one idea everything else depends on}

An AI model is basically a gigantic pile of numbers. Ours would be a very big pile: \textbf{744
billion of them}. Far too big to keep somewhere fast and convenient.

\vspace{0.5em}
\keybox{\textbf{Think of it as a library.} The model is a library with about \textbf{19,000 books}.
To produce even one word of an answer, it does not read the whole library --- it fetches a small
handful of books, reads them, and puts them back.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small]
\node[draw=green!45!black, thick, fill=green!14, rounded corners, minimum width=3.1cm,
      minimum height=1.5cm, align=center] (a) at (0,0) {\textbf{On the desk}\\tiny, instant};
\node[draw=blue!55!black, thick, fill=blue!12, rounded corners, minimum width=3.1cm,
      minimum height=1.5cm, align=center] (b) at (4.2,0) {\textbf{On the shelves}\\big, quick};
\node[draw=black!45, fill=black!6, rounded corners, minimum width=3.1cm,
      minimum height=1.5cm, align=center] (c) at (8.4,0) {\textbf{In the basement}\\huge, slow};
\node[font=\scriptsize, text=black!60, below=2pt of a] {a few books};
\node[font=\scriptsize, text=black!60, below=2pt of b] {\textbf{all 19,000 fit here}};
\node[font=\scriptsize, text=black!60, below=2pt of c] {we avoid this};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
\goodbox{\small \textbf{Good news, and the reason this project is possible at all:} our computers
have enough shelf space for \emph{the entire library}. The assistant never goes to the basement
while answering you.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Why it is slow: 600 trips to the shelf, per word}

Here is the whole speed problem, and there is no trick that makes it go away.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small, >={Stealth[length=2.5mm]}]
\node[draw=black!40, fill=black!5, rounded corners, minimum width=2.4cm, minimum height=1.1cm,
      align=center] (w) at (0,0) {\textbf{one word}};
\node[draw=blue!55!black, thick, fill=blue!12, rounded corners, minimum width=3.4cm,
      minimum height=1.1cm, align=center] (l) at (4.6,0) {\textbf{75 floors}\\to walk};
\node[draw=blue!55!black, thick, fill=blue!12, rounded corners, minimum width=3.4cm,
      minimum height=1.1cm, align=center] (b) at (9.6,0) {\textbf{8 books}\\on each floor};
\draw[->, thick] (w) -- (l);
\draw[->, thick] (l) -- (b);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.6em}
\keybox{\Large 75 floors $\times$ 8 books = \textbf{600 book-fetches for every single word.}}

\vspace{0.8em}
Everything about the design comes from trying to make those 600 trips faster. We can shorten the
walk. We can put the most-used books nearer. We \textbf{cannot} avoid the trips --- that is simply
how this kind of model works.

\vspace{0.6em}
{\small And the fetching is only \emph{half} the work. The other half is the thinking it does once
it has the books. Even if we made the fetching instant, we would only be about \textbf{twice} as
fast. Worth knowing before anyone promises ten times.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The obvious fix that does not work}

{\small \textbf{The obvious idea:} if eight people ask at the same time, fetch everyone's books in
\emph{one} trip. Serve eight for the price of one. It fails, and the reason is simple:}

\keybox{\small \textbf{Eight people want eight \emph{different} sets of books.} One trip for 8 books
becomes one trip for about \textbf{57} --- and you are still only serving eight people.}

\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{People at once} & \textbf{Books to fetch} & \\
\midrule
1 & 8 & \\
4 & 33 & barely better than doing them one by one \\
8 & 57 & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.4em}
{\small \textbf{This was actually tested}, not guessed. With eight people the group finished no
faster in total --- and each person waited \textbf{about five times longer} than going alone.}

\vspace{0.4em}
\goodbox{\small \textbf{The conclusion:} a giant model cannot serve a crowd quickly. For a crowd you
need a \textbf{smaller} model. That is why we build more than one.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{So: three helpers, not one}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small, >={Stealth[length=2.5mm]}]
\node[draw=black!40, fill=black!7, rounded corners, minimum width=2.4cm, minimum height=1.3cm,
      align=center] (u) at (-0.6,0) {\textbf{you}\\(and colleagues)};
\node[draw=blue!55!black, very thick, fill=blue!12, rounded corners, minimum width=6.4cm,
      minimum height=1.6cm, align=center] (a) at (5.8,1.3)
      {\textbf{THE EVERYDAY HELPER}\\quick, clever, \emph{serves everyone at once}};
\node[draw=green!45!black, very thick, fill=green!14, rounded corners, minimum width=6.4cm,
      minimum height=1.6cm, align=center] (b) at (5.8,-1.2)
      {\textbf{THE SPECIALIST}\\the giant model --- slow, deep, \emph{one person at a time}};
\draw[->, very thick] (u.east) -- node[font=\scriptsize, above, sloped] {you talk to this one} (a.west);
\draw[->, very thick, green!45!black] (a.south) -- node[font=\scriptsize, right, align=left]
      {and it asks the\\specialist when\\a question is hard} (b.north);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.4em}
\keybox{\small \textbf{Like a good office.} You talk to the capable colleague who is always there.
When something genuinely difficult comes up, \emph{they} ask the world expert down the hall and come
back with the answer.}

\vspace{0.3em}
{\small A \textbf{third} helper runs overnight, grinding through big piles of paperwork --- scoring
thousands of transcripts. Nobody talks to it.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Helper 1 --- the everyday one}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{How big} & about a third the size of the specialist --- still very large \\
\textbf{Where it lives} & our one machine with four graphics cards \\
\textbf{How many people} & \textbf{everybody, at the same time} \\
\textbf{How fast} & roughly \textbf{a page of text every 20--30 seconds} \\
\textbf{What it is for} & \textbf{almost everything you do all day} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.8em}
\keybox{\textbf{Why this one can serve a crowd and the specialist cannot:} it is small enough to
live entirely on the graphics cards, which are enormously faster than the shelves. The book-fetching
stops being the problem, so serving several people at once genuinely does work here.}

\vspace{0.8em}
{\small We have not picked exactly which model yet --- that is the first real job. It has to fit on
the four cards, be good at code, and be able to use tools. \textbf{It is not the small model that
was tried and rejected earlier}: this one has roughly eight times as many numbers in it and runs on
much better software.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Helper 2 --- the specialist}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{How big} & \textbf{744 billion} --- the biggest thing we can run at all \\
\textbf{Where it lives} & one ordinary machine, using most of its memory \\
\textbf{How many people} & \textbf{one at a time.} Others wait in line \\
\textbf{How fast} & roughly \textbf{a page of text per minute} \\
\textbf{What it is for} & the hard question you would otherwise be stuck on \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.7em}
\keybox{\textbf{One catch worth planning around.} Waking it up means reading 372 gigabytes off the
shared storage. That takes about \textbf{27 minutes}. So we wake it in the morning and leave it
awake, rather than starting it on demand.}

\vspace{0.6em}
{\small It is slow because it is enormous, and it is worth having \emph{because} it is enormous.
A minute of waiting for a genuinely hard answer is a good trade. A minute of waiting to rename a
variable is not --- which is exactly why helper 1 exists.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Being a good neighbour}

These are shared computers. Other people in the lab are running their own work on them, and our
assistant would be sitting on a fair chunk of them all day.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small, >={Stealth[length=2.5mm]},
  st/.style={draw=black!45, fill=black!6, rounded corners, minimum width=2.5cm,
             minimum height=1.5cm, align=center},
  hot/.style={draw=orange!70!black, thick, fill=orange!15, rounded corners,
              minimum width=2.5cm, minimum height=1.5cm, align=center}]
\node[st]  (a) at (0,0)    {\textbf{1}\\a colleague's\\job is stuck};
\node[hot] (b) at (3.2,0)  {\textbf{2}\\finish the\\answer in progress};
\node[hot] (c) at (6.4,0)  {\textbf{3}\\hand the\\computer back};
\node[st]  (d) at (9.6,0)  {\textbf{4}\\wait --- do not\\grab it again};
\node[st]  (e) at (12.8,0) {\textbf{5}\\restart once\\they are running};
\draw[->, thick] (a)--(b); \draw[->, thick] (b)--(c); \draw[->, thick] (c)--(d); \draw[->, thick] (d)--(e);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
\keybox{\small \textbf{Step 4 is the one that matters.} Without it the system hands a computer back, notices
a second later that it is short of computers, and grabs the same one straight back --- beating the
very colleague it was trying to help. It would look rude while running code written to be polite.}

\vspace{0.4em}
{\small It switches itself off when nobody has used it for a few hours, and back on when someone
does. It restarts itself if it crashes. Nobody babysits it.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{How we will know whether it is any good}

{\small Speed we can measure. \textbf{Whether it is \emph{smart} enough is harder} --- and we have
an unusually good way to test it.}

\keybox{\small \textbf{Use this project as the exam.} Planning this system turned up three real
discoveries about our own hardware that nobody had noticed. We know the answers now. Hand the local
assistant the same starting point and see how many it finds on its own.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}cl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Would we bet on it?} & \textbf{The discovery it has to make} \\
\midrule
maybe & one of our job queues can silently freeze the assistant mid-answer \\
probably & asking for memory quietly charges us for twice the computers \\
\textbf{probably not} & \textbf{why serving eight people at once cannot work} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
{\small \textbf{Grade this hardest:} does it \emph{hold its ground} when told it is wrong? One that
folds and invents a better-sounding answer is worse than one that never spotted the problem.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{What to expect: six people log in on Monday morning}

\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.36\textwidth}p{0.58\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Question} & \textbf{Honest answer} \\
\midrule
Can six people use it at once? & \textbf{Yes.} All six talk to the everyday helper, all at the same
time, and it is built for exactly that. \\
How fast for each of them? & \textbf{A page of text in 20--30 seconds.} It does not noticeably slow
down as colleagues join. \\
How does that compare to Claude? & Claude does that page in about \textbf{9 seconds}. So: two to
three times slower. \textbf{Noticeable. Not painful.} \\
What if two people need the specialist? & They \textbf{queue}. It serves one at a time, about a
minute a page, so the second person waits about two minutes. \\
First thing Monday? & The specialist needs \textbf{27 minutes} to wake up. We wake it before people
arrive. \\
Will it be as clever as Claude? & \textbf{For everyday work, close.} For long, tangled problems
across many files --- \textbf{no}, and we should not pretend otherwise. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.3em}
{\scriptsize Everything above is an estimate built from real measurements on similar machines. The
first thing we build is the test that replaces these numbers with ours.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The bottom line}

\goodbox{\large \textbf{Six people. Their own terminal. A page of text every 20--30
seconds.\\[0.25em] A genius down the hall for the hard ones.\\[0.25em] And all of it allowed to read
the data.}}

\vspace{0.7em}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
{\small\textbf{What we are promising}
\begin{itemize}\small
\item Everyone can use it at once
\item Two to three times slower than Claude
\item Good enough for everyday work
\item Nothing ever leaves the building
\item Nobody has to babysit it
\end{itemize}}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
{\small\textbf{What we are not promising}
\begin{itemize}\small
\item It will not match Claude on hard problems
\item The specialist serves one person at a time
\item It uses a real share of the lab's computers
\item Every number here still needs confirming
\end{itemize}}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{center}
\textbf{Slower. Not as clever. Allowed in the room where the data is.}

{\small That last one is the only reason to build it --- and it is enough.}
\end{center}

\end{frame}
