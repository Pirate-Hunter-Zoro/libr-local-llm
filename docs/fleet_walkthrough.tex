%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The idea, in one sentence}

\goodbox{\large Put an AI assistant \textbf{on the lab's own computers}, so several people can use
it at once --- and so it is allowed to read patient data, which no outside service ever will be.}

\vspace{1.0em}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
{\large\textbf{Today}}\\[0.4em]
{\small You use Claude. It is excellent and fast. But every word you send leaves the building, so
it can never touch a patient recording or transcript.}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
{\large\textbf{What this would add}}\\[0.4em]
{\small An assistant living on our own machines. A bit slower. Not quite as clever. But it can look
at the actual data, because nothing ever leaves.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{1.0em}
\keybox{This is \textbf{not} a replacement for Claude. It is a second tool that can go where Claude
is not allowed to go.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{First, the one idea everything else depends on}

\keybox{\textbf{The model is not one brain. It is about 19,000 narrow specialists.}\\[0.3em]
One knows about Python. One knows about Italian grammar. One knows about chemistry names. Nobody
knows everything.}

\vspace{0.5em}
{\small To write each word, the model picks the handful of specialists that word needs and
\textbf{reads their notes}. Reading the notes is the work; everything else is quick. So the design
question is: \emph{where do we keep 19,000 sets of notes?}}

\begin{center}

\node[draw=green!45!black, thick, fill=green!14, rounded corners, minimum width=3.4cm,
      minimum height=1.4cm, align=center] (a) at (0,0) {\textbf{instant}\\graphics card};

\node[font=\scriptsize, text=black!60, below=2pt of c] {we avoid this};

\end{center}

\vspace{0.4em}
\goodbox{\small \textbf{The reason this project is possible at all:} our machines have enough main
memory to hold \emph{every one} of the 19,000 sets of notes. The assistant never has to go to slow
storage while it is answering you.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Why it is slow: 600 specialists consulted, per word}

Writing one word is not one lookup. The model works through the question in \textbf{75 rounds}, and
in each round it consults \textbf{8} specialists.

\vspace{0.6em}
\keybox{\Large 75 rounds $\times$ 8 specialists = \textbf{600 sets of notes read,\\for every single
word.}}

\vspace{0.8em}
That is the entire speed problem, and no clever engineering removes it. \textbf{Reading notes is the
work.} We can keep the notes somewhere faster --- that is what the previous slide was about --- but
we cannot read fewer of them.

\vspace{0.7em}
{\small And reading is only \emph{half} the job. The other half is the thinking the model does once
it has the notes in front of it. So even if reading were free, we would only be about \textbf{twice}
as fast. Worth knowing before anyone promises ten times.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{This is the \emph{giant} model's problem}

\keybox{\large You still have to read \textbf{everybody's} notes.\\[0.3em]
Working together would only avoid slowing anyone down if you all wanted the \textbf{same} notes ---
and you never do.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Asking the giant model} & \textbf{your wait for a page} \\
\midrule
alone & about 1 minute \\
with 3 colleagues also asking & about 2--3 minutes \\
\textbf{with 7 colleagues also asking} & \textbf{about 5 minutes} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
{\small Measured, not estimated. The group does get through about \textbf{1.7 times} more work than
taking turns --- but that small gain is split eight ways while your own answer arrives \textbf{five
times later}.}

\vspace{0.4em}
\goodbox{\textbf{So the giant model is a one-at-a-time resource.} It is not the thing a crowd
should be talking to. \textbf{The next slide is why a smaller model escapes this entirely.}}

\end{frame}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule

\vspace{0.6em}
{\small It is slow because it is enormous, and it is worth having \emph{because} it is enormous.
A minute of waiting for a genuinely hard answer is a good trade. A minute of waiting to rename a
variable is not --- which is exactly why helper 1 exists.}

\begin{frame}{How good is the specialist, really?}

The honest answer depends entirely on \emph{what kind} of question you ask.

\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.26\textwidth}p{0.24\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{What you ask} & \textbf{Versus Claude} & \textbf{How long} \\
\midrule
``Here is a function and a bug report --- what is wrong?'' & \textbf{genuinely close} &
1--4 minutes \\
``Which of these two designs is better, and why?'' & \textbf{genuinely close} & 1--4 minutes \\
``Read these eight files and find the inconsistency'' & \textbf{not close} & several minutes
before it even starts \\
``Keep working on this with me for an hour'' & \textbf{don't} & see the next slide \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.4em}
\keybox{\small \textbf{One hard question, with everything it needs already in front of it.} That is
what the specialist is for, and there it is worth the wait. When the job becomes ``hold a lot in
your head and notice something subtle'', the gap opens wide.}

\vspace{0.3em}
{\small \textbf{An awkward twist:} the harder the question, the more it thinks before answering ---
and thinking is what it is slow at. The questions most worth asking are the ones you wait longest
for.}

\end{frame}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{``Can I just write down what I want and let it code?''}

{\small Yes --- with the everyday helper, and with limits worth knowing first.}

\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{A moderate coding job, start to finish} & \textbf{How long} & \textbf{Sensible?} \\
\midrule
Claude, today & 3--5 minutes & yes \\
\textbf{the everyday helper} & \textbf{6--12 minutes} & \textbf{yes} \\
the specialist & about 30 minutes & no --- leave it alone \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
\keybox{\small \textbf{Two to four times slower than Claude for the same job.} Write the
description, start it, go and do something else for ten minutes. A real workflow, not a demo.}

\vspace{0.3em}
{\scriptsize \textbf{The catch is not about speed.} To edit your files the model fills in a little
form for each action, and because we run a \emph{compressed} copy it \textbf{sometimes fills the
form in wrong}. There is a built-in repair for the simple forms --- open a file, run a command ---
but \textbf{not for the complicated ones}, and ``change this line to that line'' is a complicated
one.}

\vspace{0.3em}
\goodbox{\scriptsize \textbf{Start with small, clearly described jobs and check the result.} How
often it fills the form in wrong is one of the first things we measure --- if it is often, ``hand it
a spec'' is a demo, and we want to know that early.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{``Wait --- do we need to pay for an API key?''}

\goodbox{\large \textbf{No.} Nothing is bought, nothing is billed, and no word you type ever
reaches Anthropic or anyone else outside the building.}

\vspace{0.5em}
{\small There are two things called a ``key'' here, and only one is real.}

\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.26\textwidth}p{0.66\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{The fake one} & The Claude software refuses to start unless a certain box has \emph{something}
in it. So we type the word \texttt{local}. It is not an account, it is not checked by anyone, and it
never leaves the machine. It is a seatbelt light, not a ticket. \\[0.5em]
\textbf{The real one} & \textbf{A password of our own}, that we choose, protecting our own
assistant. This one matters. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
\keybox{\small \textbf{Why a password at all:} these are \textbf{shared} computers. Anyone else
logged in could otherwise walk up and use our assistant --- spending our share of the lab's
computing time, and asking it things we would be answerable for.}

\vspace{0.4em}
{\small \textbf{One specific hazard:} conversations sit in numbered slots. If a stranger picks the
number you are using, you do not get two conversations --- you get one, tangled together.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Being a good neighbour}

{\small Everything on the previous slide happens \textbf{by itself}. Nobody negotiates, nobody
emails, nobody has to remember to switch anything off. \textbf{You do nothing.}}

\vspace{0.4em}
\vspace{0.5em}
\keybox{\small \textbf{The subtle one is ``waits''.} Without it the system hands a computer back,
notices a second later that it is short of computers, and grabs the same one straight back ---
beating the very colleague it was trying to help. It would look rude while running code written to
be polite.}

\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
notices a colleague is stuck & \textbf{automatic} \\
\textbf{finishes the answer you are waiting on first} & \textbf{automatic} --- it never cuts you off mid-sentence \\
hands the computer back, waits, takes it again later & \textbf{automatic} \\
switches off when unused, back on when needed & \textbf{automatic} \\
restarts itself after a crash & \textbf{automatic} \\
reconnects you when it moves to a different computer & \textbf{automatic} \\
holds your question while it is starting up & \textbf{automatic} --- you see ``starting'', not an error \\
\midrule
\textbf{switch it off right now} & \textbf{the only button you ever press} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How we will know whether it is any good}

{\small Speed we can measure. \textbf{Whether it is \emph{smart} enough is harder} --- and we have
an unusually good way to test it.}

\keybox{\small \textbf{Use this project as the exam.} Planning this system turned up three real
discoveries about our own hardware that nobody had noticed. We know the answers now. Hand the local
assistant the same starting point and see how many it finds on its own.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}cl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Would we bet on it?} & \textbf{The discovery it has to make} \\
\midrule

\textbf{probably not} & \textbf{why serving eight people at once cannot work} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
{\small \textbf{Grade this hardest:} does it \emph{hold its ground} when told it is wrong? One that
folds and invents a better-sounding answer is worse than one that never spotted the problem.}

\end{frame}
